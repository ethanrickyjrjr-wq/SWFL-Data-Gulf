## 1. What a buyer's agent must now put in writing (NAR's own pages)

**Primary source:** [NAR — "Written Buyer Agreements 101"](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/written-buyer-agreements-101), fetched via crawl4ai 08/12/2026.

Verbatim from the page:

> "As of August 17, 2024, an MLS Participant 'working with' a buyer is required to enter into a
> written agreement with the buyer prior to touring a home, including both in-person and live
> virtual tours."

**Mandatory provisions** (NAR's own heading), quoted verbatim — "Pursuant to paragraph 58(vi) of the
NAR proposed settlement agreement, written buyer agreements must":

> - "Specify and conspicuously disclose the amount or rate of any compensation the MLS Participant
>   will receive from any source, or how this amount will be determined;
> - The amount of compensation must be objectively ascertainable and may not be open-ended (e.g.,
>   'buyer broker compensation shall be whatever amount the seller is offering to the buyer');
> - Include a statement that MLS Participants may not receive compensation from any source that
>   exceeds the amount or rate agreed to with the buyer;
> - Disclose in conspicuous language that broker commissions are not set by law and are fully
>   negotiable; and
> - Include any provisions required by law."

NAR also states what its policy will **not** dictate (verbatim): "Type of relationship the
professional has with the potential buyer... Term of the agreement... Services to be provided...
Type or amount of compensation charged." So the mandatory written content is narrow — compensation
amount/rate, its ceiling, negotiability, and legally-required provisions — everything else (scope of
services, term, exclusivity) is optional/negotiated, not NAR-mandated.

**Confirming hub page:** [NAR — "Get the Facts"](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts), fetched
08/12/2026, lists the same August 17, 2024 effective date and links "Consumer Guide to Written Buyer
Agreements," "Consumer Guide to Open Houses and Written Agreements," and "Consumer Guide: Offers of
Compensation" as the companion consumer-facing pages, plus a member-facing "Guide to Settlement
Practice Changes for Real Estate Professionals" PDF. The same page confirms MLS compensation display
is the other headline change (title of the compensation consumer guide: "As a home seller, you can
choose to offer—or authorize your agent to offer—compensation to a buyer's agent... " — i.e.,
compensation now moves off the MLS listing and into a direct offer/negotiation).

**Bottom line, factually stated:** the one thing NAR's rules put IN WRITING for a buyer's agent is
the **compensation term** — a specific, ascertainable, capped, negotiable number, disclosed before
the first tour. Everything else in a buyer agreement (scope of service, duration, exclusivity,
termination) is left to state law and broker/consumer negotiation, not mandated by NAR.

## 2. 2025–2026 brokerage/coaching guidance on demonstrating value

Three independent sources, each published in 2025–2026, each specifically about the post-settlement
period:

### Source A — Jamil Academy (real estate coaching, Top 1% producer)

[jamilacademy.com — "How to Justify Your Commission: Buyer Agent Value Scripts (2026)"](https://www.jamilacademy.com/blog/how-to-justify-your-commission-buyer-agent-scripts),
published May 4, 2026, fetched via crawl4ai 08/12/2026.

Verbatim:

> "If you are a buyer's agent trying to justify your commission in 2026, the honest answer is that
> you do it in writing, before the first showing, with a scope-of-work document that spells out
> exactly what your fee buys."

> "When I present the agreement, I bring a one-page list of deliverables with me, and I walk the
> buyer through it before we ever discuss the number." The listed deliverables include
> "Pricing analysis, so you never overpay in a market where the list price is a marketing number,
> not an appraisal" and "Offer strategy and drafting, including contingencies that protect your
> deposit."

> "How do I justify my commission to a buyer in 2026? Show the buyer a written scope of work before
> they sign... Justification is a conversation about value backed by a document, not a defense of a
> percentage."

This source ties the mandatory NAR compensation disclosure to a broader, non-mandatory practice: a
written "deliverables list" agents are coached to hand buyers, of which pricing/comps-backed
guidance and offer strategy are named line items.

## 3. The deliverable question: is "here is the sourced reasoning behind this offer" a documented pain point?

**Answer: NOT FOUND as a distinct, named pain point in any source fetched for this task.**

What the sources document, precisely:

- The **named, survey-measured pain point** (Source B, Cotality/ResiClub via Krista Mashore) is
  **"buyer-side compensation negotiations"** — i.e., agents struggling to have the fee/commission
  conversation, not agents struggling to produce or document the reasoning behind an offer price.
- The **coached deliverable** that comes closest to "sourced reasoning behind an offer" is Source A's
  "Pricing analysis" and "Offer strategy and drafting" line items on the written deliverables list —
  but these are framed as *services buyer's agents should list to justify their fee*, not as a
  friction point agents report struggling with. No source states that agents lack the ability to
  produce comps-backed offer reasoning, or that buyers are asking for it and not getting it.
- A separate, general (non-settlement-specific) search on CMA practice turned up language about a
  buyer-side CMA "show your work" comps grid as a 2025–2026 trend in CMA-tooling marketing copy
  (bounti.ai, theclose.com), but the bounti.ai page failed to load via crawl4ai (`'NoneType' object
  has no attribute 'raw_markdown'` — recorded per the no-substitute-for-memory rule) and
  theclose.com's fetched text did not contain the "show your work" phrase on the buyer-agent angle —
  so this thread is **not corroborated** well enough to count as a hit on the question asked, and is
  reported here only as a lead, not a finding.

**What this means for the commercial-wedge decision (factual, not a recommendation):** the evidence
gathered supports that buyer's agents have a documented, survey-measured struggle with the
**compensation/fee conversation**, and are being coached to solve it with a **written
deliverables/scope-of-work list** that includes pricing analysis as one line item among many
(representation, access, negotiation, vendor coordination, deadline management). It does **not**
support that "sourced reasoning behind this specific offer" is, on its own, a named pain point
agents are asking for help with. Any product-wedge argument built on that specific framing would be
extrapolating past what these sources say — the closest honest claim the evidence supports is
narrower: a written, comps-backed offer-strategy artifact would fit inside the "deliverables list"
buyer's agents are already coached to produce, as one line among several, not as the fix for the pain
point actually measured in survey data.
